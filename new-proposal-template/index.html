Dear Laura and Sara,

You are not deciding whether to change the name. You are finding out whether the name and the identity are getting in the way of your fundraising.

Thank you for the note last week. The way you framed it is right, and it changes what we would propose.

Those are two different questions, and the second one has to be answered first. So we rebuilt this around that. Phase 1 is an assessment with one fee, and it answers one question with evidence: is the current name and identity helping S:US raise money, holding it back, or doing neither.

If the answer is that things are working, you will have spent a modest amount to learn that and you can stop there. If the answer is that something needs to change, you will walk into the board conversation with findings instead of an opinion.

Phase 2 is where naming and brand exploration happen. Phase 3 is where the identity gets built. Neither one makes sense until Phase 1 is done.

We were also glad to hear that another team is building your case for support, and that messaging comes with it. That work should come first. Assessing a brand before the messaging is settled would mean guessing at what the brand is supposed to carry, so the sequence you described is the right one.

One thing we would raise. You mentioned board members and senior leadership, and you were not sure about donors. Since the question is about fundraising, donors are the people who can answer it most directly. We would want a few of them among the eight conversations.

— The Liminal Team

02

One question, answered with evidence

S:US wants to know whether its name and visual identity are helping it raise money or getting in the way. Right now that question gets answered by opinion, and opinions differ.

01

A question, not a conclusion →

Nothing has been decided. The assessment is built to support either answer, including the one where the current name stays.

02

Two names, one organization →

People often read S:US as the name of the organization. Services for the UnderServed is the full name. Part of the work is understanding which one is doing the heavy lifting with donors.

03

It has to work in the board room →

A recommendation that rests on taste will not survive that conversation. One that rests on what donors and board members actually said will.

03

Ask the people closest to the money

The assessment is built around the audiences whose behavior the question is really about.

A

Talk to the people who give and the people who ask

Eight conversations across board members, senior leadership, and donors. The donor conversations are the ones that answer the fundraising question most directly.

B

Look at the brand where it actually lives

The website, donor communications, materials, and social. How the name and identity behave in the places a funder encounters them.

C

Read it against peers

How comparable organizations in New York and nationally present themselves to the same funders. Detrimental is a comparative word, so the answer needs a comparison.

D

Build on the messaging work, not beside it

We use what your case for support partner produces as the standard the brand is measured against.

06

Three phases, one at a time

Start with the assessment. Decide after. Nothing downstream begins until the first phase has earned it.

Phase 1. Name and Brand Identity Assessment

$12,000
6 to 8 weeks
What happens
  • A review of how the name, logo, and identity are used today across the website, donor communications, materials, and social.
  • Interviews with up to eight people, drawn from board members, senior leadership, and donors.
  • A peer review of how comparable organizations present themselves to the same funders.
  • A screen of the current name: how people search for it, what else uses it, and what the digital footprint looks like.
  • Review of the case for support and messaging work your other partner is producing, used as the standard we measure the brand against.
  • A findings report with a clear recommendation and the reasoning behind it.
  • A presentation of the findings to your leadership team.
What you get
  • Brand Assessment Report
  • Interview Findings Summary
  • Peer Review
  • Recommendation and Rationale
  • Leadership Presentation

Phase 2. Naming and Brand Exploration

If the assessment calls for it
$18,000
10 to 12 weeks
What happens
  • Name development grounded in the Phase 1 findings, including the option to keep the current name and use it differently.
  • Testing with internal audiences and a donor sample.
  • A URL and social handle check on the shortlist.
  • Early visual identity directions that follow from the name decision, enough for your board to react to.
  • A recommendation and the case for it, built for the board conversation.
What you get
  • Name Candidates with Screening Results
  • Visual Identity Directions
  • Recommendation and Rationale
  • Board Presentation Materials

Trademark screening through counsel is billed at cost and is not included above.

Phase 3. Logo, Brand Identity, and Brand Guidelines

$22,000
12 to 14 weeks

Your brand is the visual representation of your organization's essence and a key identifier for your constituents and community. Our team, in partnership with you, will develop an identity that reflects your desired brand tone and distinguishes your organization from others in your field.

The logo process, in three rounds
  • Initial presentation of three distinct concepts.
  • Refinement round focused on the selected direction.
  • Final round to refine and prepare the production-ready logo system.

The identity

  • Logo, type treatment, color palette, and brand fonts.
The visual style guide
  • Proper logo usage and logo variations
  • Typography pairings
  • Color palette breakdowns for web and print
  • Social media profile images

Templates for presentations, letterhead, and division materials can be quoted separately once the system is built.
