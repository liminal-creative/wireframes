02 — What We Noticed

Four Hypotheses, Not Findings

These came from a close read of your site and your RFP. We hold them loosely. The point of an assessment is to test them against what your team and your community actually say.

01

The language is interchangeable

Proven approaches. Customized support. Rolling up sleeves. Every firm in your category says a version of this. None of it tells a fundraiser what working with Aperio feels like.

02

The proof is stated, not felt

Two hundred thirty partners and a team of former fundraisers is a strong position. Right now it reads as a credential rather than the reason someone should call you.

03

The name is carrying nothing

Aperio is Latin for "I open" or "I reveal." That is a good name for what you do. Nowhere does the brand let anyone in on it.

04

The visual system says growth while the words say relationships

The mark reads as literal growth. Your copy is about partnership and trust. The palette is calming, and parts of it likely fall short of accessibility standards on screen.

05

The energy lives in the sub-brand

Fearless Fundraisers has more personality than the parent brand. Worth understanding whether your community knows you by the name on the invoice or the name in their feed.

06

One word to test

Philanthropy reads from the funder's side. Your buyers are fundraisers. We want to know whether that word helps you or quietly puts distance between you and the people you serve.

We are sharing these upfront because you should know how we think before you hire us. Some will hold. Some will not survive the first round of interviews.
03 — Our Approach

Messaging First, Then Everything Else

We work in three moves. Clarify what is true, design how it shows up, activate it across the organization. An assessment lives almost entirely in the first move, which is where most brand work goes wrong when it gets skipped.

Phase 01

Immersion and insight

Interviews inside and outside the firm, a survey of your community, a peer scan, and a review of what you already put in front of buyers.

Phase 02

Brand foundation

We synthesize what we heard into positioning, differentiators, and proof points that your team recognizes as their own words.

Phase 03

Recommendations and roadmap

Prioritized guidance, usable outputs you can apply immediately, and a sequenced plan for what comes next.

04 — Scope

What We Assess

Your RFP named seven areas. Here is how we would cover them.

Internal clarity

  • Interviews with your leadership team, client services, and business development.
  • Selected members of your Expert Network, who see the brand from the outside in.
  • Where your team agrees on what Aperio is, and where the definitions quietly diverge.

Community perception

  • Interviews with current partners, past partners, and trainees.
  • A short survey to your partner base, built for a high response rate rather than a long instrument.
  • Prospects who chose someone else, if you can open that door for us.
  • A defined priority audience profile, drawn from what we hear rather than assumed at the start.

Peer assessment

  • A landscape review of boutique fundraising consultancies competing for the same conversations.
  • How each one positions, what they claim, and which claims are now table stakes.
  • The open ground where Aperio can stand without arguing.

Messaging, channels, and sales materials

  • Website, email, social, and print reviewed for consistency and clarity.
  • Proposals, decks, and case studies reviewed for whether they move a relationship forward.
  • Your events, trainings, and sponsorships assessed as brand touchpoints rather than marketing line items.
  • The prior emotional market research study read alongside what we hear now, to see what shifted.
05 — Deliverables

What You Receive

Assessment synthesis and recommendations

  • Key findings across internal clarity and external perception.
  • Audience themes and what they mean for growth.
  • Peer benchmark and the differentiators that survive it.
  • Prioritized recommendations for positioning and messaging.
  • Channel and sales enablement gaps, with investment guidance and return considerations.
  • A near-term roadmap and a longer-term plan to inform the refresh.
  • A decision process for applying the brand going forward, including who approves what.

Outputs you can use the week we finish

  • A refreshed core narrative and message map, including an elevator pitch and proof points.
  • Messages tailored to each priority audience.
  • A practical brand guide covering voice, tone, and visual basics with real examples.
  • Website recommendations you can act on now, including priority pages and content architecture.
Every deliverable is presented live to your team. A document nobody discusses is a document nobody uses.

07 — Optional Phase 2

Budgetary Estimate for the Refresh

You asked for a planning number, not a commitment. Based on the scope your RFP anticipates, a full refresh built on the assessment would run $60,000 to $80,000. That range assumes the following.

  • A complete messaging guide extending the assessment narrative into every audience and channel.
  • Visual identity refresh, including the mark, palette, and type system, with accessibility resolved.
  • A brand guide your team and outside vendors can both work from.
  • Website refresh built on the architecture recommended in the assessment.
  • Template systems for proposals, decks, social, email, and event materials.
The range narrows once the assessment tells us how far the identity needs to move. If the words carry the weight, this lands near the bottom. If the visual system needs rebuilding, it lands near the top.
