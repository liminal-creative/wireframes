01 — Introduction

Dear Aperio team,

We read your RFP a couple of times, and we spent a long time on your website as well.

The thing that stood out to us is this. Everyone on your team has actually done the work of fundraising. You have been responsible for a portfolio, set the goals, and felt what it is like when the money has to come in. Most firms that sell fundraising advice have never been in that seat. You have. That is why your partners trust you, and it is the best thing you have going.

Here is our honest reflection. We read your whole website and still wondered what you actually do. The thing that makes you different is in there. It is just not the thing anyone notices first.

That gap is worth understanding before anyone touches a logo or a homepage. So this proposal is built around two questions. What does your own team believe Aperio is? And what do the 230 organizations you have worked with say when someone asks them about you? Those two answers rarely match as closely as people expect, and the space between them is usually where the real work is.

You spend your days helping nonprofits figure out how to talk about themselves. Being on the other side of that is a lot harder than it looks. We know, because we have had to do it for ourselves.

Looking forward to hearing from you.

The Liminal Team

02 — What We Noticed

Six Observations

These came from reading your site and your RFP. They are starting points, not conclusions. The assessment either confirms them or replaces them with what your team and your community tell us.

01

Your website could be any firm’s website

Proven approaches. Customized support. Rolling up sleeves. Every fundraising consultancy says a version of this. None of it tells a fundraiser what it is like to work with you.

02

The best thing about you is a footnote

Every consultant on your team has carried a portfolio. That is the reason to call you. Right now it sits in a paragraph on the About page.

03

Nobody knows what Aperio means

We looked it up, but most people won’t. There is probably something meaningful behind the Latin for “I open” or “I reveal.” We’d love to explore this together.

04

The logo and the copy tell different stories

The mark shows leaves. The writing is about trust and partnership. The palette is calm, and some of it may be hard to read on a screen.

05

Fearless Fundraisers has more life in it

The sub-brand sounds like people. The parent brand sounds like a firm. Worth finding out which name your community says out loud.

06

One word we would test

Philanthropy is a word funders use. The people who hire you are fundraisers. We want to know whether that word draws them in or holds them at arm’s length.

This is what we see from the outside. We’d love to get closer to learn things about Aperio we don’t know yet.
03 — Our Approach

We Get the Words Right First

Every brand project we run starts with what is true, then moves to how it looks, then to getting it used. An assessment is almost all of the first part. Skipping it is how organizations end up with a beautiful website that still does not explain what they do.

Phase 01

Listening

We talk to your team and to the people who hire you. We survey your community, look at your competitors, and read everything you put in front of a prospect.

Phase 02

Finding the through line

We turn what we heard into language your team recognizes. Who you are, what you do, and why it matters, written so anyone at Aperio can say it the same way.

Phase 03

What to do about it

Recommendations in priority order, language you can start using right away, and a plan for what comes after this engagement.

04 — Scope of Work

Exactly What We Would Do

Your RFP named seven areas. Here is the work, step by step, so there are no surprises about what is included.

01. Kickoff strategy session

  • A 90-minute working session with your leadership team.
  • We get clear on the goal behind this project. Not the brand goal, the business goal. What is growth supposed to look like in three years, and what is in the way right now.
  • We build the interview list together and agree on who we are talking to and why.
  • You hand over your materials, including the market research study you already have.

02. Qualitative interviews

  • Questionnaire developed by us and approved by you before anyone is contacted.
  • Ten interviews over Zoom, roughly 45 minutes each, split between your team and your community.
  • Your side: leadership, client services, business development, and selected members of your Expert Network.
  • Their side: current partners, past partners, trainees, and prospects who hired someone else.
  • Scheduling and reminders handled by us, not by your team.
  • A themed audience interview report. Not a transcript dump. Written by the person who sat in the interviews.

03. Community survey

  • A short survey to your partner base. Short enough that people finish it.
  • Built to test what we hear in the interviews against a bigger group.
  • You help with the outreach, which you already offered, and that is what makes the response rate worth something.

04. Peer assessment

  • Six to eight boutique fundraising consultancies going after the same work.
  • What each one claims, and which claims every firm now makes.
  • A visual and verbal comparison so you can see where you sit and what is left to say.

05. Search and AI visibility scan

  • What comes back when a development director asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Google for help finding a fundraising consultant.
  • Whether your site gives those tools anything to work with, and what to change so it does.
  • Good messaging no longer helps if machines cannot read it and repeat it.

06. Materials and channel review

  • Website, email, social, and print, read for whether they say the same thing.
  • Proposals, decks, and case studies, read for whether they help you win the work.
  • Your events, trainings, and sponsorships, which shape the brand as much as the website does.

07. Brand strategy

  • Mission, vision, pillars, and values, written out and agreed on.
  • Voice, so your team writes like one firm instead of nineteen people.
  • A new About Us and a boilerplate you can use everywhere tomorrow.
  • Before and after, so you can see what changed and why.
05 — Deliverables

What You Get

A findings and recommendations report

  • What your team said, and what your community said.
  • The patterns in those answers, and what they mean for growth.
  • How you compare to your competitors, and which of your differences survive the comparison.
  • What to fix, in the order we would fix it, written as from and to rather than as themes.
  • Where your channels and sales materials fall short, what it would cost to fix them, and what you get back.
  • A plan for the next few months and a longer plan for the refresh.
  • Who approves brand decisions going forward, so this does not drift again.

Your brand strategy, written out

  • Mission, vision, pillars, values, and voice on a single page you can hand to anyone.
  • A new About Us and boilerplate, ready for your website and your proposals.
  • The proof points behind each claim, so nobody has to invent them later.
We present all of it live to your team. A document nobody talks about is a document nobody uses.
