01 — Introduction

Dear Aperio team,

We read your RFP a couple of times, and we spent a long time on your website as well.

The thing that stood out to us is this. Everyone on your team has actually done the work of fundraising. You have been responsible for a portfolio, set fundraising goals, and you understand what it feels like when the money has to come in. Most firms that provide fundraising advice have never been in that seat. You have. That is why your partners trust you, and it is the best thing you have going.

But here is our honest reflection. We read through your whole website, and we still wondered what you actually do.

We also want to be honest with you about money, since you were honest with us when we reached out with our questions. What you laid out in the RFP includes seven aspects of your brand you want us to examine and two full sets of deliverables. If we were to do that thoroughly, it would be closer to $68,000 than $25,000. We are not going to tell you we can do all of it for less and then ignore the aspects that would make it worth your investment.

So, we wanted to present two options. One is in the range you shared and focuses on fewer things, but would still be done well. The other covers everything included in the RFP. Both are priced to allow us to actually deliver on what we promise. You let us know that this will inform how you plan your budget, so we wanted to give you a real number you can plan around rather than only providing something in your range just to try to get the contract.

One more thing. You spend your days helping nonprofits figure out how to talk about themselves. Being on the other side of that is a lot harder than it looks. We know, because we have had to do it for ourselves, and we understand how important that is.

Looking forward to hearing from you.

The Liminal Team

02 — What We Noticed

Six Observations

These came from reading your site and your RFP. They are starting points, not conclusions. The assessment either confirms them or replaces them with what your team and your community tell us.

01

Your website could be any firm’s website

Proven approaches. Customized support. Rolling up sleeves. Every fundraising consultancy says a version of this. None of it tells a fundraiser what it is like to work with you.

02

The best thing about you is a footnote

Every consultant on your team has carried a portfolio. That is the reason to call you. Right now it sits in a paragraph on the About page.

03

Nobody knows what Aperio means

We looked it up, but most people won’t. There is probably something meaningful behind the Latin for “I open” or “I reveal.” We’d love to explore this together.

04

The logo and the copy tell different stories

The mark shows leaves. The writing is about trust and partnership. The palette is calm, and some of it may be hard to read on a screen.

05

Fearless Fundraisers has more life in it

The sub-brand sounds like people. The parent brand sounds like a firm. Worth finding out which name your community says out loud.

06

One word we would test

Philanthropy is a word funders use. The people who hire you are fundraisers. We want to know whether that word draws them in or holds them at arm’s length.

This is what we see from the outside. We’d love to get closer to learn things about Aperio we don’t know yet.
03 — Our Approach

We Get the Words Right First

Every brand project we run starts with what is true, then moves to how it looks, then to getting it used. An assessment is almost all of the first part. Skipping it is how organizations end up with a beautiful website that still does not explain what they do.

Phase 01

Listening

We talk to your team and to the people who hire you. We survey your community, look at your competitors, and read everything you put in front of a prospect.

Phase 02

Finding the through line

We turn what we heard into language your team recognizes. Who you are, what makes you different, and the proof that backs it up.

Phase 03

What to do about it

Recommendations in priority order, materials you can start using right away, and a plan for what comes after this engagement.

04 — Scope

Our Approach

Your RFP named seven areas. Here is how we would cover them.

What your own team says

  • Interviews with your leadership team, client services, and business development.
  • Members of your Expert Network, who see Aperio from the outside.
  • Where your team gives the same answer about what Aperio is, and where the answers split.

What your community says

  • Interviews with current partners, past partners, and trainees.
  • A short survey to your partner base. Short enough that people finish it.
  • Prospects who hired someone else, if you can open that door for us.
  • A clear picture of who you should be talking to, based on what we hear.

What your competitors say

  • A review of the boutique fundraising consultancies going after the same work.
  • What each of them claims, and which claims everyone now makes.
  • What is left for you to say that nobody else is saying.

What your materials say

  • Website, email, social, and print, read for whether they say the same thing.
  • Proposals, decks, and case studies, read for whether they help you win the work.
  • Your events, trainings, and sponsorships, which shape the brand as much as the website does.
  • The market research study you already have, read against what we hear now.
05 — Deliverables

What You Get

A report on what we found

  • What your team said, and what your community said.
  • The patterns in those answers, and what they mean for growth.
  • How you compare to your competitors, and which of your differences hold up.
  • What to fix, in the order we would fix it.
  • Where your channels and sales materials fall short, what it would cost to fix them, and what you get back.
  • A plan for the next few months and a longer plan for the refresh.
  • Who approves brand decisions going forward, so this does not drift again.

Things you can use the week we finish

  • Your core story written out, with an elevator pitch and the proof to back it up.
  • Versions of that story for each audience you care about.
  • A short brand guide covering how you sound and how you look, with real examples.
  • Website changes you can make now, including which pages matter most and how to organize them.
We present everything to your team. A document nobody refers to is a document nobody uses.
06 — Investment

Two Options

Option A fits the range you shared. It does less, and we tell you what it leaves out. Option B is the work your RFP describes. We recommend Option B and will do good work either way.

Option A: Foundational Assessment

Option B: Full Assessment

The work your RFP describes • 14 weeks

Everything above, plus: 12 interviews with your team and 15 with your community. Conversations with your Expert Network and with prospects who hired someone else. A look at eight competitors. A full review of your channels and your sales materials. Your mission, vision, services, values, and voice written out. A brand guide. Website recommendations. A plan for now and a plan for the refresh. An approval process. Two presentations. Jesica D’Avanza leading the research.

Either option: 30% at signing, with the rest invoiced in equal monthly payments. If you want any of this done in person, we bill travel at cost and ask you first.
07 — Optional Phase 2

A Number for Your Budget

You asked for a planning number, not a commitment. Based on what your RFP describes, a full refresh built on the assessment would run $60,000 to $80,000. That range assumes the following.

  • A messaging guide that carries the new story into every audience and channel.
  • An updated logo, color palette, and typefaces, with the contrast problems fixed.
  • A brand guide your team and any outside vendor can both work from.
  • A website rebuilt on the structure we recommend in the assessment.
  • Templates for proposals, decks, social posts, email, and event materials.
The range tightens once we know how far the look needs to move. If the words do most of the work, this lands near the bottom. If the logo and palette need rebuilding, it lands near the top.
