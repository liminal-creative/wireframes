02 — The Challenge

The Challenge

The foundation has a clear new focus and no shared language for it, which makes every conversation about who you are start from scratch.

The mission narrowed, the words did not

Your board spent eighteen months getting to upward mobility at the transition out of high school. The public description of the foundation still says help people to help themselves.

Five directors, five versions

David named this himself. Ask all five and you get five stories. That costs you every time a director describes the foundation to a prospect or a peer funder.

Nowhere to send anyone

A search turns up a short paragraph on someone else's site. A prospective director or a partner foundation has no way to check you out before the meeting.

The Solution

05 — How We Work

Messaging First, Then Design

James said the board expects a logo, a positioning statement, and a narrative. We agree on all three. We would change the order they arrive in.

A logo drawn before the language is settled is a guess. When we hand our creative director a finished message set, the identity has something to be about. So Phase One produces the positioning statement and the narrative. Phase Two turns them into a mark. The design work starts partway through Phase One, so the calendar does not stretch.

The four spaces

  • Sidewalk. One sentence. Someone decides in a hallway whether to ask a second question.
  • Front porch. Thirty seconds. The version for a peer funder who wants more.
  • Living room. The bet you are making, what you fund, and what you have seen work.
  • Kitchen table. Directors, prospective directors, and anyone weighing a gift to the foundation.
Your board is your first audience. If the story does not settle five directors, it will not settle anyone else.
06 — Phase One

Brand Strategy & Core Messaging

Eight weeks • Weeks 1 to 8

We interview your directors and the people closest to the work, then reflect back what we hear. The first draft usually lands about eighty percent of the way there. We get to one hundred with you, line by line.

Discovery

  • Written questionnaires and one-on-one conversations with all five directors, plus Dara.
  • Conversations with two to four grantees, chosen with you. Southeast Raleigh Promise and a current transition-focused grantee would be useful.
  • An outside-in read of how the foundation currently appears to someone searching for it.

The messaging guide

  • Positioning statement and brand narrative.
  • Interpretation of the founder's mission in language the board can stand behind.
  • Vision and values, plus a defined brand voice.
  • The challenge you address and the opportunity you fund.
  • Sidewalk, front porch, living room, and kitchen table versions of the story.
  • Target audiences, with what moves each one.
  • Language for grants and for program related investments, so both read as one strategy.
  • Lexicon. The words you use and the ones you avoid.
  • A one-page reference sheet for directors, pulled from the full guide.
The one-pager is the piece your board will actually use. The full guide is what we design and write from.
07 — Phase Two

Visual Identity & Brand Guidelines

Six weeks • Begins week 5, overlapping Phase One

Your creative brief comes out of the messaging work. Our creative director builds from it. You have almost nothing to undo, which is an advantage a rebrand with decades of existing material would not have.

Logo and identity

  • Three initial directions, then one refinement round, then production files.
  • A wordmark that resolves how the foundation is named in short form and in full.
  • Typography pairings for print and screen.
  • A color palette with web and print breakdowns.

Brand guidelines

  • Logo usage and variations, plus clear space rules.
  • Type and color specifications.
  • Imagery direction, including how you show the young people you fund without turning them into subjects.
  • Letterhead and email signature, plus a simple grant award template.
08 — Phase Three

Website

Ten to twelve weeks • Begins after messaging sign-off

David said you do not need a push strategy. You need somewhere for people to land when they hear your name. We agree, and we would build for that rather than for traffic.

Approach

  • Built on WordPress with a page builder Dara can manage without a developer.
  • Copy written by us from the approved messaging, not placeholder text you have to fill in.
  • Mobile first, and fast.
  • Few plugins, each one vetted.
  • Two rounds of revision at wireframe and at design.

Deliverables

  • A six to eight page site. Likely pages: home, what we fund, how we work, our history, the board, and contact.
  • A custom home page template plus three to five unique page templates.
  • Clear language on how grants are sourced, so a stranger understands the invitation model without having to ask.
  • Training so your team can update text and images.
  • Baseline SEO setup and an indexing check.
  • Premium hosting and on-call maintenance for the first three months after launch.
