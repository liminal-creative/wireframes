01 — Introduction

Dear James, David, and Dara,

Thank you for the quality time we spent with you on August 26. We came away from that conversation feeling like your foundation is at a pivotal moment, and we would love the opportunity to work with you.

Your foundation has been doing incredible work for over six decades, and you have done a lot of good over the past year and a half to clarify your path forward. You took a founder’s charge to help people to help themselves, which could describe almost any grant to almost any organization, and you narrowed it to something people can understand. The words you use across the board have not yet caught up to that decision. That is what stands out most to us. From our perspective, you are not looking for a mission. You already found one. You are looking for language that is clear enough that all of your current and future directors can tell the same story. And you are looking for a website to send people to when they want to know who you are before they connect with you personally.

With this in mind, we would help you with three things:

  • Messaging that unearths who you are, what you do, and why it matters.
  • A visual identity and name treatment that reads as serious to peer funders and to the next generation of directors.
  • A website that answers the questions that a first-time visitor has.

Our work is solely focused on nonprofits and foundations like yours. Southeast Raleigh Promise, United Way of the Greater Triangle, Abara, and the Boys & Girls Clubs of the Greater Triad are among the organizations we have worked closely with.

We would love the opportunity to be considered as your partner in establishing a public presence that your current directors and future board members can be proud to be a part of.

We look forward to hearing from you.

02 — The Challenge

The Challenge

The foundation has a clear new focus but no shared language for it, which means every conversation about who you are starts from each director’s perspective.

The mission is focused, but the words are not

Your board spent 18 months getting to a point where you were focused on upward mobility as students transitioned from high school. The public description of the foundation still says “helping people to help themselves.”

A different version from each director

David named this. If we were to ask all five, we would get five different stories. That has a cost associated with it every time a director describes the foundation to a prospect or a peer funder.

Nowhere to send people

A search turns up a short paragraph on another website. A prospective director or a partner foundation has no way to check you out before a follow-up conversation.

03 — The Solution

The Solution

We start with language, because the identity and the website are both downstream of it. Here is what we would build with you.

A core messaging stack

Positioning, narrative, audiences, program language, and the words you use and avoid. One source of truth that the board works from.

The positioning statement and narrative James asked for

This would be the first thing to emerge from the first phase of our work together.

A visual identity built from the messaging

A logo, fonts, and color system would be included in a comprehensive brand guide designed with messaging as the foundation.

A website that gives you the credibility you are looking for

Six to eight pages. Enough to answer real questions, small enough that a board without staff can keep it current.

04 — What Changes

A Year From Now

Here is what should feel different.

Recruiting

You’re sitting with a board candidate

Instead of explaining the foundation from the beginning, you send them a link beforehand. They show up already interested.

Grantmaking

A request comes in that’s close to what you fund, but not quite

You compare it to your newly established messaging. Everyone is on the same page based on what you have already agreed upon.

Peer funders

Someone finds you without an introduction

A foundation working on the same transition looks you up and sees an overlap. Right now, they have no way of knowing if you are truly coming from the same perspective.

05 — How We Work

Messaging First, Then Design

James said the board expects a logo, a positioning statement, and a narrative. We agree on all three. We propose that they arrive in a different order.

A logo designed before the language is established is a best guess. When we hand our creative director a finalized brand messaging document, the visual identity can be developed from what the words have already established to be true.

Phase One produces the positioning and the narrative. Phase Two turns them into a visual identity. Phase Three is a website that makes it all come together.

The four spaces

  • Sidewalk. One sentence. People opt in or opt out.
  • Front porch. Thirty seconds. The version that draws people in and makes them want to learn more.
  • Living room. The stories you tell about impact through the organizations you fund.
  • Kitchen table. Directors, prospective directors, major funders, and partners.
06 — Phase One

Brand Strategy & Core Messaging

Four to Eight weeks • Weeks 1 to 8

We interview your directors and the people closest to the work, then reflect back on what we hear. The first draft usually lands about eighty percent of the way there. We get to one hundred with you, line by line.

Discovery

  • Written questionnaires and one-on-one conversations with all five directors, plus Dara.
  • Conversations with two to four grantees, chosen with you. Southeast Raleigh Promise and a current transition-focused grantee would be useful.
  • An outside-in read of how the foundation currently appears to someone searching for it.

The messaging guide

  • Positioning statement and brand narrative.
  • Interpretation of the founder’s mission in language that the board can stand behind.
  • Vision and values, plus a defined brand voice.
  • The challenge you address and the opportunity you fund.
  • Sidewalk, front porch, living room, and kitchen table versions of the story.
  • Target audiences, with what moves each one.
  • Language for grants and for program-related investments, so both read as one strategy.
  • Lexicon. The words you use and the ones you avoid.
  • A one-page reference sheet for directors, pulled from the full guide.
The one-pager is the piece your board will actually use. The full guide is what we design and write from.
07 — Phase Two

Visual Identity & Brand Guidelines

Six weeks • Begins week 5, overlapping Phase One

Your creative brief comes out of the messaging work. Our creative director builds from it. You have almost nothing to undo, which is an advantage that a rebrand with decades of existing material would not have.

Logo and identity

  • Three initial directions, then one refinement round, then production files.
  • A wordmark that resolves how the foundation is named in short form and in full.
  • Typography pairings for print and screen.
  • A color palette with web and print breakdowns.

Brand guidelines

  • Logo usage and variations, plus clear space rules.
  • Type and color specifications.
  • Imagery direction, including how you show the young people you fund without turning them into subjects.
  • Letterhead and email signature, plus a simple grant award template.
08 — Phase Three

Website

Ten to twelve weeks • Begins after messaging sign-off

David said you do not need a push strategy. You need somewhere for people to land when they hear your name. We agree, and we would build for that rather than for traffic.

Approach

  • Built on WordPress with a page builder, Dara can manage without a developer.
  • Copywritten by us from the approved messaging, not placeholder text you have to fill in.
  • Mobile first, and fast.
  • Few plugins, each one vetted.
  • Two rounds of revision at the wireframe and design phases.

Deliverables

  • A six to eight-page site. Likely pages: home, what we fund, how we work, our history, the board, and contact.
  • A custom home page template plus three to five unique page templates.
  • Clear language on how grants are sourced so a stranger can understand the invitation model without having to ask.
  • Training so your team can update text and images.
  • Baseline SEO setup and an indexing check.
  • Premium hosting and on-call maintenance for the first three months after launch.
