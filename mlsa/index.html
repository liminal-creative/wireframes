01 — Introduction

Dear Hilary and the MLSA team,

We're excited about the opportunity to help Montana Legal Services Association mark this next chapter.

Sixty years of free civil legal services to Montanans who could not otherwise afford them is a milestone few organizations reach, and a brand that hasn't kept pace over the years is a solvable challenge.

Your mission statement already makes it clear who you're for as you work to protect and enhance the civil legal rights of, and promote systemic change for, Montanans living in poverty. The challenge is that the rest of the brand doesn't live up to that level of clarity yet, and that gap is exactly what we'd love to work with you to close.

We understand this RFP as an invitation to help MLSA decide, clearly and purposefully, who it is now: an organization reaching more people across a larger and more diverse state than it did decades ago. And you want people to know that you are still rooted in the same commitment to access and dignity. The work is to make sure your name, your voice, your messaging, and your visual identity match the quality of your programming.

These are the very things we care about.

At Liminal, our approach is grounded in these three words: Clarify, Design, Activate. If we have the privilege of working with you, we will help you clarify your messaging, elevate your work with the professional design you deserve, and help you activate your brand with an engaging website that inspires funders to support your mission.

This proposal responds to each requirement in your RFP, followed by our approach, timeline, team, and pricing. We look forward to talking it through with you.

03 — Discovery & Research

Research before recommendations

We will open the engagement with structured discovery: real interviews and surveys, a full asset audit, and a scan of the field, so recommendations rest on what we find rather than a guess.

We've already started. MLSA's current site uses at least four different taglines side by side: "Montana's Solution to Unmet Legal Aid Needs," "Equal Access to Justice," "Fighting Poverty. Achieving Justice. Improving Lives.," and "Justice Lives Here." That reads to us like an organization that has grown faster than its messaging, and closing that gap is exactly what this project can do.

  • Up to six brand interviews and surveys with leadership, including Executive Director Alison Paul, board members, funders, and a sample of current and former clients, conducted in a way that respects confidentiality.
  • A full audit of existing brand assets: MLSA's current logo and print materials, MontanaLawHelp.org, and any regional program branding.
  • A Peer Landscape Analysis showing how other legal aid and access-to-justice organizations position themselves, in Montana and nationally, so we can find where MLSA's story stands apart.
  • A findings memo naming the gap between how MLSA operates today and how its brand currently reads, with clear recommendations for what should change.
04 — Renaming Analysis

An honest approach on the name

Deciding whether to change MLSA's name is strategic work, grounded in research rather than a logo refresh. MLSA already considers systemic reach a core strategy: case selection focuses on issues affecting many people, and programs like MontanaLawHelp.org are built to reach entire populations rather than one client at a time.

We'll evaluate the current name against three questions: does it describe what MLSA does in language a person in crisis would use, does it create confusion or baggage in the communities MLSA is trying to reach, and does it work alongside MontanaLawHelp.org.

If a new name tests well, we'll propose two or three directions, each checked for availability and how each is perceived by funders, partners, and the courts. If the current name should stay, we'll tell you, and we'll put that energy into messaging and visual identity instead. Either way, you get branding expertise and an honest recommendation with the reasoning behind it.

We'll present two concept directions so staff and the board can react to real options together, then decide on one system. Once a direction is chosen, we will build it out completely.

  • A PowerPoint presentation template

06 — Messaging & Positioning

Language your team approves before anything gets designed

In our process, messaging always comes before design, so the identity work above would be built on language that MLSA approves. Deliverables include core brand message and tagline, a positioning statement, a set of storytelling options staff can pull from directly, and dedicated brand transition messaging for the people who have known MLSA for decades.

Key messages will speak directly to the audiences MLSA already works alongside: the courts, tribal governments and communities served through the Tribal Advocacy Incubator Project, and the universities, foundations, and government partners who fund and refer clients to MLSA's work.

07 — Website Redesign & Development

A site built for the people you serve

We'll design and build a new MLSA website that puts client access first. MLSA's current site already gives donors, people seeking help, and volunteers three distinct paths in. We'll keep that structure and strengthen it, while closing a real access gap: the HelpLine hours, Tuesday through Thursday, 9 AM to 1 PM, leave most of the week without live phone support, so the new site needs stronger self-service resources for people who need help outside that window.

  • Full design and development of the new site
  • CMS selection based on MLSA's staff capacity and long-term maintenance needs
  • Content migration from the current site
  • On-site search
  • Spanish-language content support
  • Accessibility built to WCAG 2.1 AA guidelines, tested with assistive technology as well as automated scanners
  • Quality assurance and cross-browser testing before launch
  • Documentation and hands-on training for MLSA's website editors
08 — Launch & Implementation

A rollout your partners and funders can follow

We'll build a rollout plan that sequences the announcement across MLSA's own channels, partner organizations, funders, and local media, with messaging that explains why the change happened and what stays the same: MLSA's mission and the people it serves.

The plan includes implementation guidance so MLSA's team knows what to update and when, from email signatures to court filings.

09 — Project Methodology

Clarify, Design, Activate

Liminal engagements move through three phases. This structure lines up directly with MLSA's own milestones for discovery, visual identity, the brand style guide, and the implementation plan.

Clarify

Phase 2

Design

The visual identity, brand collateral, and the new website, built on what Clarify confirmed.

Phase 3

Activate

Launch planning, staff training, and the transition itself.
